Day 1 · Saturday, August 15, 2026

Arrival Day 🚆

Eurostar to Gare du Nord (13:09) → check in → Montorgueil dinner

Settle in 🛋️ Easy evening 🌆 Public holiday 🇫🇷
📍 Tonight's Neighbourhood in Maps↗
🇫🇷
Aug 15 is Assumption Day — a French public holiday. Paris is lovely and quiet (locals are away). Big sights stay open, but some small shops close and mid-August is when many restaurants take summer holidays. Tourist-area spots (Rue Montorgueil, Marais) will be open — good call for tonight.

🧭 Getting to the Hotel

You arrive Paris Gare du Nord at 13:09 — the hotel is a short hop away.

🚇 Cheapest & quick — Metro M4
Direct from the platforms: M4 Gare du Nord → Réaumur–Sébastopol (4 stops, ~8 min), then a 2-min walk. ~€2.55/adult + child fare.
🚇 Live route
🚕 Easiest with luggage — Bolt / Uber
~€10–14, about 10 min. Worth it after 3+ hours on the train with a tired 7-year-old and bags. Taxi rank is right outside Gare du Nord too.

Day 3 · Monday, August 17, 2026

Montmartre & Grand Boulevards 🎨

Hilltop village → Opera palace → Arc de Triomphe sunset

Full day ⚡ Opera on a Monday ✅ Jungle dinner 🌴
📍 Open Full Day Route in Maps↗
🎭
Opera is on today for a reason. Monday gives you the best shot at seeing the actual auditorium at Palais Garnier — Sunday matinees often close it off. Do Montmartre first thing (before the crowds and heat), then work your way down the boulevards.

🧭 Getting Around Today

🚇 To Montmartre: M4 → Barbès-Rochechouart → M2 → Anvers, then the funicular up the hill (uses one metro fare). Or a Bolt straight to the top (~€13) to skip the climb with Nirvaan. Live route
🚕 Montmartre → Opera: short Bolt (~€10) or M12 down the hill. Palais Garnier + Galeries Lafayette rooftop are 2 min apart, all on foot.
🚆 Opera → Arc de Triomphe: RER A from Auber → Charles de Gaulle–Étoile (2 stops, direct). Live route
🚕 Arc → dinner → home: Bolt across to Jungle Palace in the 10th (~€16), then a €10 hop home. A car is worth it once everyone's tired.

💡 This is the biggest walking day — a day-pass (Navigo Jour €12.30) pays off if you'll take 5+ rides. Otherwise tap contactless.

☀️ Paris in August — Good to Know

🌡️Warm, 25–30°C, sometimes a heatwave. Hats, sunscreen, refillable water bottles (public fountains everywhere). Metro can get hot — carry water.
🏖️Many Parisians are away — the city is calmer, but some restaurants close for summer holidays. Have a backup dinner option each night.
🇫🇷Aug 15 (arrival day) is a public holiday — sights open, some small shops shut.
🌅Long days — sunlight until ~9:30 PM. Great for late-afternoon sightseeing without rushing.
👜Keep bags zipped and to the front in crowds (Montmartre steps, Trocadéro, metro). Petty pickpocketing is the main annoyance.

🧒 Winning With a 7-Year-Old

🎠Carousels are everywhere — Tuileries, near the Eiffel Tower, Sacré-Cœur, Hôtel de Ville. Cheap, always a hit.
⛵Toy sailboats at the Tuileries pond = the best €4 you'll spend all trip.
🍦Build in ice-cream/crêpe stops as "rewards" between the grown-up bits (Berthillon, Montorgueil).
😴Plan one sit-down break each afternoon. A tired 7-year-old at 5 PM is the real trip-killer — Bolt home rather than push it.
